# USMLE Step 1 — Modules 4 and 5 authoring review

Authored and checked on 9 October 2026 using the project's [question-writing recipe](USMLE_Question_Writing_Recipe.html).

## Scope and review status

Modules 4 and 5 each contain 15 original questions. Each block includes 12 mechanism, prediction, pharmacology, or localization items, one diagnosis item, one communication item, and one biostatistics item. Each has three informative tables and five options per question. The 20-item recipe's mix is scaled to the requested 15-item blocks; it is not an official examination blueprint.

All 30 items contain a tested source proposition, clinical or study stem, focused lead-in, key, general explanation, five paired option rationales, learning objective, reasoning chain, and references. Stems are 72–90 words before the lead-in and any table. Original scenarios were written from the scientific concepts, without copying textbook cases, images, or existing question-bank vignettes. Tables supply required information rather than decoration. No image licensing is required for these original text-and-table items.

**Author source and construction review is complete. Independent physician/subject-matter review has not been performed and remains outstanding under the recipe.** The bank records this explicitly in each new item's `reviewStatus`. These are educational Step 1-style items, not official examination questions or independently clinically validated material.

## Construction and scientific audit

- Clinical items were checked for a coherent clue-to-process-to-endpoint chain. Lead-ins identify an answer category without requiring choices to supply the question. Options address the same endpoint, and each distractor has a specific exclusion rationale.
- No negative lead-ins, all/none-of-the-above options, or duplicate options. Answer positions are shuffled deterministically after authoring, with each letter correct three times per module. Each rationale remains paired with its option.
- Topic, discipline, key, and explanation appear only in the post-answer teaching section. The source proposition and review status are authoring metadata, not pre-answer cues.
- Module 4/6 explains that foot-process effacement is not unique to minimal change disease; age, complement, renal function, and sediment establish the intended context.
- Module 4/10 specifies three weeks after trauma so upper motor neuron findings do not conflict with acute spinal shock.
- Module 4/11 does not treat abnormal DHR as absolutely specific for NADPH oxidase deficiency; the MPO distractor explanation acknowledges assay overlap and uses the infection phenotype.
- Module 4/12 distinguishes pulmonary loss of antiprotease protection from hepatic retention of abnormal protein.
- Module 5/3 avoids unsafe dehydration testing in an already hyperosmolar patient; the paired findings and supervised desmopressin response identify renal resistance.
- Module 5/8 identifies all-trans retinoic acid as one component of a treatment regimen, not monotherapy.
- Module 5/10 uses preserved inversion, hip abduction, and sole sensation to distinguish a common fibular lesion from L5 or tibial injury.
- Module 5/13 states the usual sporadic complete-mole mechanism and acknowledges rare recurrent biparental moles. The p57 stain includes a positive maternal internal control.
- Communication questions contain direct patient statements and ask for the next response. Study scenarios use original hypothetical counts and do not attribute outcomes to an actual treatment or exposure study.
- This source audit found edition-specific chapter differences: G&G autonomic neurotransmission is ch. 10, psychosis/mania ch. 19, autacoids ch. 41, anti-inflammatory drugs ch. 42, antimycobacterials ch. 65, and kinase inhibitors ch. 71. The installed references use the checked edition's numbers.

## Numerical checks

- Module 4/15: control risk = 60/1,000 = 0.06; treatment risk = 40/1,000 = 0.04; absolute risk reduction = 0.02; NNT = 50 over five years.
- Module 5/15: OR = (60 × 180)/(60 × 60) = 3.0. The ratio of exposure proportions is 2.0 and is deliberately a distractor.
- Module 5/3: urine osmolality rises from 110 to 118 mOsm/kg, a 7.3% increase. It remains inappropriate for plasma osmolality of 307 mOsm/kg.
