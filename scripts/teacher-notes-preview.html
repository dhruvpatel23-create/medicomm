General principles of antimicrobial therapy

Sheet01

When do we treat?

Primary prophylaxisprevent the first infection
Pre-emptiveearly test evidence, before symptoms
Empiriclikely pathogens; cultures pending
Definitiveculture-guided and narrower
Suppressiveprevent relapse when cure is difficult

Teacher's rule:Take meaningful cultures first—but never delay urgent therapy in sepsis or meningitis.

Choosing the right drug

  • Likely organism + local antibiogram
  • Site penetration
  • Severity and urgency
  • Allergy and pregnancy
  • Immune status
  • Renal/hepatic function
  • Toxicity + interactions
  • Previous antibiotics

Drain abscesses, debride tissue and remove infected hardware: antibiotics cannot replace source control.

When should drugs be combined?

  • Broader empiric cover in a critically ill patient
  • Proven synergy for selected organisms or sites
  • Prevent resistance when single-step escape is likely
  • Use only when benefit exceeds added toxicity, interactions and microbiome damage

Always interpret susceptibility with the dose, infection site and achievable free concentration—not as a standalone laboratory answer.

Kill or inhibit?

BactericidalDirectly kills bacteria. Favoured when host defence is limited or the site is protected.
BacteriostaticStops bacterial growth. Host immunity then completes clearance.

The distinction is useful—but organism, site, exposure and host immunity still decide the outcome.

From specimen to de-escalation

Good specimen
Gram stain + rapid test
Culture + MIC
Narrow, switch or stop

Reassess at 48–72 hours: response, cultures, organ function, route and planned duration.

Read the report:Susceptible, intermediate or resistant must be interpreted with the specimen quality and clinical response.

How resistance develops

↘Reduced entry through altered porins
↗Efflux pumps lower intracellular drug
✂Enzymes destroy or modify the drug
◎Altered target prevents useful binding
⇢Pathway bypass avoids the blocked step
≋Biofilm + persisters produce tolerant populations

Resistance spreads by mutation and horizontal transfer: conjugation, transduction and transformation.

Antimicrobial stewardship

  • Start with adequate coverage when illness is severe.
  • Review cultures early and de-escalate.
  • Use the narrowest effective spectrum.
  • Optimise dose, route, exposure and duration.
  • Combine agents only for proven synergy, resistance prevention or toxicity reduction.
  • Avoid duplicate coverage and unnecessary prophylaxis.
  • Protect the microbiome and reduce C. difficile risk.

If treatment is failing, ask why

  • Wrong organism, resistance or a noninfectious diagnosis
  • Inadequate dose, absorption or penetration into the site
  • Undrained focus, necrotic tissue, biofilm or infected device
  • Poor adherence or profound immune dysfunction

Final exam line → right drug + right dose + right site + source control + early review.
